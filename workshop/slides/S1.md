# How a bank decides on a small business

## Originate → Adjudicate → Price → Monitor → Manage

| Stage | Question | Who answers it today |
|---|---|---|
| Adjudicate | Do we say yes? | Scorecard + policy rules |
| Price | At what rate is yes *worth it*? | An ROE engine — no ML at all |
| Monitor | Who is quietly getting worse? | Behavioral early-warning |
| Manage | Who deserves more credit? | Candidate model + economics |

# PD: the one number everything shares

**Probability of Default** — the chance this business fails to repay in the next 12 months.

- Banks score the **business**, not the loan — one entity, many decisions
- Every module you'll build consumes the *same* score spine
- A 300–850 score is just PD wearing a friendlier suit

> **Signature idea #1:** the leverage is in the **application**, not the model.
> You will watch modeling get *simpler* every session while business value goes *up*.

# Why synthetic data — and why banks care

**Tonight you'll generate 12,000 businesses that never existed.**

- **Privacy:** zero real customers → safe for a public repo, safe for your portfolio
- **Control:** we *choose* the ground truth, so we can measure what models *should* find
- **Teachability:** identical seed 42 → your numbers match these slides exactly

> Identical numbers across 30 laptops is perfect for a classroom —
> and would be **alarming in production**. Hold that thought for discussion.

# Anatomy of a data-generating process

```
attributes  →  latent risk (logit)  →  + noise  →  Bernoulli draw  →  label
 (industry,      (weights we chose)     (on purpose!)                (default: 0/1)
  DSCR, ...)
```

Three design rules you will defend tonight:

1. Observable features drive a **latent logit** — with noise **injected deliberately**
2. The label is a **draw**, not a formula — perfect prediction is impossible *by design*
3. Truth columns are **written down and denied** to every model

<!-- Speaker: the noise is the point — S1's oracle-ceiling block and S4's escalation
     story both grow from this slide. -->

# Leakage: the six forbidden columns

`shared/config.py::LEAKAGE_COLUMNS` — **named, central, reviewable:**

| Column | Why it's poison |
|---|---|
| `pd_default_origination` | the DGP's ground-truth PD |
| `default` | the answer itself |
| `risk_based_rate` | priced *from* the true PD |
| `booked` | a post-decision outcome |
| `deterioration_next_6_12mo` | S4's target |
| `line_increase_good` | S4's other target |

Every feature-engineering module **asserts** against this list. A deny-list in someone's head is not a control; a deny-list in `config.py` is.

# The oracle ceiling

**If the DGP injects noise, even perfect knowledge has a maximum score.**

Before you ever set a metric gate, measure the ceiling two ways:

1. **Noise-free latent** — score the clean logit itself against the drawn labels
2. **Oracle model** — strongest model, observable features only, held out

Your gate must sit **below both**. Otherwise you've ordered the impossible.

> In the reference build, an agent proved a required AUC was mathematically
> unreachable — and **refused to fake it**. Twice. Session 4 tells both stories.

<!-- Speaker: this is the single most transferable idea of the program. On synthetic
     data the ceiling is measurable; on real data it still exists — you just can't see
     it, which is worse. -->
