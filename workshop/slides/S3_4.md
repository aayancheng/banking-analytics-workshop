# A decision is not a score

The score estimates risk. The **policy layer** decides how the bank acts on that estimate.

1. **Hard knockouts** — a non-negotiable failure declines the applicant before the model
   gets the final say.
2. **PD zones** — low PD starts in Approve, middle PD starts in Refer, high PD starts in
   Decline.
3. **Refer overrides** — affordability, score, or request-size concerns can downgrade an
   approval to Refer; they do not upgrade a Decline.

> Policy is where risk appetite becomes a customer decision.

# Policy parameters banks actually use

| Family | Typical parameters | Decision role |
|---|---|---|
| Affordability | DSCR, interest coverage, free cash flow | Can cash flow support the debt? |
| Credit history | bureau score, prior delinquencies, public records | Has the borrower repaid reliably? |
| Leverage & capacity | leverage, debt-to-equity, request / revenue | Is the exposure too large for the business? |
| Collateral & recovery | LTV, collateral value, guarantees, lien position | How much loss might recovery reduce? |
| Exposure controls | total relationship, industry/region caps | Is the portfolio becoming concentrated? |
| KYC / AML / fraud | identity, ownership, sanctions, fraud alerts | Must the application stop or escalate? |

The workshop makes these visible through `dscr_floor`, `prior_delinq_cap`, `leverage_cap`,
`req_to_rev_cap`, `score_floor`, and the PD-zone thresholds.

Exact values are product-specific. They are calibrated to risk appetite, portfolio evidence,
regulation, and validation—not copied from a universal checklist.

# ROE and RAROC — pricing is where the money happens

```text
interest income
− cost of funds
− expected loss (PD × LGD)
− operating cost
= pre-tax profit
→ tax → net income
→ net income ÷ allocated equity = ROE
```

**ROE:** after-tax net income ÷ allocated equity. Does the loan earn the bank’s required
return on capital?

**RAROC:** pre-tax profit ÷ allocated equity. How attractive is the risk-adjusted economics
before tax?

The workshop capital allocation is simplified: `allocated equity = 12% × EAD`.

# Quoted rate, hurdle rate, recommended rate

```text
break-even rate
  = cost of funds + PD × LGD + operating cost − fees

hurdle-clearing rate
  = break-even rate + after-tax capital return requirement

recommended rate
  = hurdle-clearing rate + base margin
```

- **Quoted rate:** what the booked loan currently carries.
- **Hurdle-clearing rate:** the minimum rate needed to reach the 15% ROE hurdle.
- **Recommended rate:** the hurdle-clearing rate plus the 2% reference margin cushion.

**Business decision:** below hurdle → reprice, restructure, or do not book; above hurdle →
minimum return clears; above recommended → clears the hurdle plus the cushion.

<!-- Block 0:32–0:35 · Generalization -->

# The 54% vs 21.6% trap

The whole-book curve can show about **54% capture at 10% reviewed** while held-out capture
is **21.6%**.

- The whole-book curve includes accounts the model saw during training, so it is optimistic
  and can rank training-specific noise unusually well.
- The held-out red point uses unseen accounts and is the honest estimate of future workflow
  performance.
- Leakage-safe features prevent direct cheating; held-out evaluation checks generalization.

> Quote the held-out number. A model can look brilliant on the book it learned from and
> still be ordinary on tomorrow’s book.

The gate uses held-out capture ≥ 20% because operational capacity—not an attractive curve—
determines whether the watchlist is useful.

# Precise line-increase offer rules

An offer is a **conjunctive gate**. Every condition must pass:

```text
candidate probability ≥ 0.3121
AND origination PD ≤ 0.0741
AND recommended amount > $0
AND incremental ROE ≥ 15%
```

The recommended amount is the smallest of three limits:

```text
min(
  amount needed to reach 65% utilization,
  50% of current credit limit,
  30% of annual revenue − current credit limit
)
```

The candidate probability asks “is this a good growth candidate?” The origination PD asks
“is this account inside risk appetite?” Incremental ROE asks “is the additional exposure
worth funding?”
