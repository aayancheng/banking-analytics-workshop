7 / 13Anatomy of a data-generating process

Anatomy of a data-generating process

attributes  →  latent risk (logit)  →  + noise  →  Bernoulli draw  →  label
 (industry,      (weights we chose)     (on purpose!)                (default: 0/1)
  DSCR, ...)

Three design rules you will defend tonight:

  1. Observable features drive a latent logit — with noise injected deliberately
  2. The label is a draw, not a formula — perfect prediction is impossible by design
  3. Truth columns are written down and denied to every model

10 / 13The oracle ceiling

The oracle ceiling

If the DGP injects noise, even perfect knowledge has a maximum score.

Before you ever set a metric gate, measure the ceiling two ways:

  1. Noise-free latent — score the clean logit itself against the drawn labels
  2. Oracle model — strongest model, observable features only, held out

Your gate must sit below both. Otherwise you've ordered the impossible.

In the reference build, an agent proved a required AUC was mathematically unreachable — and refused to fake it. Twice. Session 4 tells both stories.
Speaker: Block 2:30–2:50 · Teach: the oracle ceiling Speaker: this is the single most transferable idea of the program. On synthetic data the ceiling is measurable; on real data it still exists — you just can't see it, which is worse.
