# Session polls — three launches, all earning their time

**Facilitator notes. Not student-facing.**

Set up in the Zoom **web portal** → your scheduled meeting → *Polls/Quizzes* → before the day.
Basic polling covers all of this; nothing here needs Advanced. **Make every poll anonymous** —
this room is bank practitioners, and the honest answers to two of these questions ("my employer
doesn't allow it", "I got stuck and missed most of it") are exactly the ones nobody gives under
their own name.

**Three launches, not six.** Each poll costs ~90 seconds once you include launching, waiting and
talking to the results — and the rehearsal already ran 22% long. Two of the three sit in time
that is otherwise dead, and the third buys something the pilot needs more than time.

**Sharing the results is the engagement, not the asking.** Launch, give it 45–60 seconds, then
*Share Results* and say what you see. A poll you collect silently is a survey.

## Poll A — "While your container builds" · launch 0:04

**The slot is free.** The Codespace takes 2–4 minutes and the run of show already says to teach
into that gap. A poll fills it better than talking does: people whose container is still
spinning have something to do, and you get the room's shape before you pitch anything.

Three questions, launched together, anonymous.

**Q1 · How much have you used AI coding agents (Claude Code, Copilot, Cursor, Codex…)?**
- Never
- Tried once or twice
- I use one most weeks
- I use one most days

**Q2 · Where do you run them?**
- In the cloud (Codespaces, a cloud IDE)
- Locally on my own machine
- Both
- My employer doesn't permit it yet
- Haven't used one

**Q3 · Which models are you using? (select all)**
- Claude — Opus
- Claude — Sonnet
- GPT / ChatGPT / Codex
- GitHub Copilot (whatever it runs — fine not to know)
- Gemini
- Local or open-weight models
- None yet

> The "fine not to know" option on Copilot is deliberate. Plenty of people genuinely don't know
> which model sits behind their tool, and without that option they either guess or skip — and
> you learn nothing either way.

**What you do with it, immediately.** If Q2 comes back heavy on *"employer doesn't permit"*, the
container block stops being about convenience and becomes the headline: *this runs in a browser,
on GitHub's machine, with nothing installed and nothing to approve.* If it comes back heavy on
*local*, lean the other way: *the container is how we guarantee the same machine for everyone
tonight.* Same block, different pitch — and the poll is what tells you which.

## Poll B — "What are you here to build?" · launch 0:14

One question, multi-select, anonymous. It lands inside the *promise of five sessions* block
because the answer legitimately changes what you promise.

**Which of these are you working on now, or expect to? (select all)**
- Credit scoring / PD models
- Adjudication or underwriting decisions
- Risk-based pricing
- Early warning / portfolio monitoring
- Model documentation, validation, MRM
- Data quality and pipelines
- Not sure yet — here to find out

The options map one-to-one onto the five sessions, so the result tells you which weeks to
weight, and gives you a line you cannot fake: *"Eighteen of you picked monitoring — that's
Session 4, and it's the one where the honest answer is that the model has a ceiling."*

## Poll C — "One question before you go" · launch 1:29

One question, anonymous. Twenty seconds.

**Tonight was:**
- Too slow — I wanted more
- About right
- Too fast — I lost the thread
- I got stuck on setup and missed most of it

⚠️ **Do not share these results on screen.** The other two you share; this one you read
afterwards. If eight people say they got stuck, putting that on the wall at 9:29pm ends the
night on the worst note available — and it makes the next person less likely to answer honestly
next week.

That fourth option is the whole reason the poll exists. It is the failure you most need to know
about and the one nobody will volunteer in chat, because saying it in front of forty peers
means admitting you spent the evening lost.
